# Learned search and preservation of prior competence

Frozen before the first run on 2026-10-18. This is an independent, bounded
implementation of established search-and-learn ideas, not a reproduction of
the original LTS-CM benchmarks or a language-model experiment.

## Question and competing mechanisms

Does reusing a learned search policy produce better subsequent learning than
keeping the search policy fixed? Does optimizing a search-effort bound help
more than ordinary action imitation? Can retaining an old complete search
process preserve coverage more effectively than mixing actions at every step?

The relevant prior work is Expert Iteration (2017), LevinTS (2018), and LTS
with context models (2023). The proposed portfolio guarantee is a standard
fair-scheduling argument. No originality claim is made for these ingredients.

## System and interventions

The environment is the 3x3 sliding-tile puzzle, goal 1..8,0. A search policy is
a legal-action softmax of additive learned contexts. Each context identifies
the blank position and the position of one named tile: 648 contexts and four
action logits, 2,592 trainable numbers. No distance oracle or hand-designed
distance heuristic is supplied. All training targets are solution paths found
by the executing search. State cuts follow the Markov/probability condition
of LevinTS; search priority is log(depth)-log(path probability).

Three routes begin from identical zero weights:

- frozen_ce: collect solutions using the initial uniform policy at every round;
- reuse_ce: collect with the most recent learned policy, fit action cross entropy;
- reuse_lts: collect with the most recent learned policy, fit regularized log
  mean search bound divided by mean path length.

All retain their cumulative solved-path dataset. Each round uses at most 120
L-BFGS-B iterations, at most 160 joint objective/gradient evaluations, bounds
[-3,3], and L2 coefficient 1e-4 on the sum of squared weights. CE is token mean
cross entropy. The alternative is log(mean(d*exp(path NLL)))/mean(d), plus the
same regularizer. It has the same unregularized minimizers as mean(d/p), but
regularization and normalization matter. Finite optimization is reported as
such, not described as an attained global optimum.

Five rounds contain 96 distinct starts each, generated by non-backtracking
random walks with length ranges [4,8], [6,10], [8,12], [10,14], [12,18]. These
lengths are generation settings, not exact solution distances. Each task has a
256-pop cap, including duplicate pops. Primary comparison seeds are 18,29,43,71.
Pilot seed 1 uses 32 starts per round; its results are retained separately.
Changes motivated by the pilot must be recorded before confirmatory runs.

## Held-out behavior and common-target update

Each seed reserves 96 starts from walks [10,16] and 96 from [18,26]. Starts
are disjoint from all training and common-target generation starts. Shared
intermediate states, especially near the goal, are allowed and expected.
These are within-domain transfer cohorts, not separate scientific domains.

Evaluate every final learned policy at 256 and 1,024 total pops under:

- learned Levin search alone;
- local mixture: 25% uniform probability and 75% learned probability at each step;
- complete-search portfolio: one uniform-search pop for every three learned
  search pops, with independent frontiers. Count every pop from both searches.

Uniform search is a baseline. The portfolio does not use validation tuning.
In a 4-step schedule it supplies each search its promised share up to an
additive constant. The guarantee concerns pops, not unmeasured equal wall time.

For a common-target comparison, each route's final actor and the uniform actor
solve the same 96 new generation starts from [10,18] at 256 pops. Their found
paths train identical zero-initialized CE learners. Evaluate these students
with the same learned-only harness at 1,024 pops on the held-out starts. This
tests a bounded data-producing improvement operation on common targets.

## Accounting and decision criterion

Preserve found paths, task identities, optimization records, learned parameters,
per-task pops, policy calls, generated edges, and process CPU seconds. Search
pop caps and optimization ceilings are common, while realized training data
volume and arithmetic differ and are reported. This is not a matched-FLOP or
matched-energy comparison. External assessment cost is reported separately.

Primary outcome: held-out fraction solved at the declared pop cap. Secondary:
capped mean pops, solution lengths, CPU time and both cohort scores. The common
target outcome is the paired difference in downstream student solved fraction.
Use paired seed-level differences; four seeds do not support precise tail
probabilities or broad performance claims. Keep all results, including failures.

An improvement in training search alone does not establish transferable actor
improvement. A portfolio need not beat the best individual search at the same
cap: its protection has a price. The objective comparison may fail because the
bound is loose, the contexts are limited, or its hard-tail weighting transfers
poorly. The route will be revised from the observed result rather than treating
any of these methods as a required winner.
